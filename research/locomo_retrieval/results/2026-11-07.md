# LoCoMo retrieval: what the 2026-11-07 run says

Raw tables are in `2026-11-07-summary.md`. Per-question rows (`results.csv`) stay out of the repo. They hold question words from a CC BY-NC dataset.

## Setup

- LoCoMo `locomo10.json`, pinned by SHA-256. 1,536 questions are scored: categories 1 to 4, minus 4 with no evidence.
- 1 fresh AtticDB store per conversation. Each turn is 1 note: `"<speaker>: <text>"`.
- A question counts as found when an evidence turn, as the dataset labels it, is in the top k. No answering model, no judge.
- The arms were fixed in `docs/plans/2026-11-07-locomo-retrieval.md` before any run.
- 1 run, seed 0, on an Apple M4 laptop. Other work kept the load average near 5. Latency is noisy, and recall doesn't depend on load.
- A second run gave identical results for all 7,680 rows (1,536 questions × 5 arms).

## Results at k = 10

| Arm | Recall | Hit | ≈ tokens returned | Search p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AtticDB, whole question | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0 | 0.12 ms |
| AtticDB, rarest word | 39.0% | 44.0% | 191 | 0.11 ms |
| AtticDB, 2 rarest words | 27.5% | 30.9% | 70 | 0.10 ms |
| SQLite FTS5, BM25 over all the question's words, OR'd | 56.1% | 61.8% | 315 | 0.17 ms |
| Random 10 turns | 2.1% | 3.1% | 327 | n/a |

The full conversation averages about 19,200 tokens (bytes/4).

## What this says

- **AND over every word is why a pasted question finds nothing.** AtticDB keeps only turns that hold every query word. A question's filler words are almost never all in 1 turn. Stripping the "?" changes almost nothing: only 3 questions then match an evidence turn.
- **The query is most of the gap to BM25, not the ranking.** BM25 here ORs all the question's content words. The 1-word arm searches 1 word.
  - The rarest word appears in an evidence turn for only about half the questions (48.6%).
  - So even a perfect ordering of its matches tops out near 50% hit, against BM25's 62%.
  - Ordering costs a few points on top. The 1-word arm reaches 44% hit at k = 10, and 46.5% at k = 20.
- **Requiring 2 words is worse than 1.** Recall drops from 39% to 28%, because the second word is often absent from the evidence turn.
- **The 1-word arm is a heuristic, not an agent.** It picks the rarest word using word counts over the whole conversation. An agent has no such counts, so it may pick better or worse words.
- **Multi-hop and open-domain questions are hard to recall in full.** They have several evidence turns. At k = 10, BM25 finds 1 for 48% of multi-hop questions. It recalls only 27% of their evidence.
- **Search is fast.** p50 is about 0.1 ms over the local Unix socket.

## Next to Living Brain

Living Brain reports 92.2% judged answer accuracy with GPT-5 ([benchmarks page](https://docs.livingbrain.com/docs/benchmarks)). It also reports 9.4K memory tokens per question and a 592 ms median retrieval. This run measures whether search returns the labelled evidence. Their score needs an answering model and a judge on top, and their page reports no full-context accuracy. They also score 1,540 questions, not 1,536. These numbers are not a head-to-head with theirs.

## What this doesn't cover

- QA accuracy. A model might recover from a near-miss turn, or fail despite the right one.
- Semantic search. Embeddings are #117.
- Session dates. They weren't stored, so "when" questions rely on the turn text alone.
- Variance across seeds. Random used seed 0 only. Every arm, random included, gave identical rows across 4 runs.
