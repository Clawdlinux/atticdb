# LoCoMo retrieval: what the 2026-11-07 run says

The raw tables are in `2026-11-07-summary.md`. The per-question rows (`results.csv`) stay out of the repo, because they hold question words from a CC BY-NC dataset.

## Setup

- LoCoMo `locomo10.json`, pinned by SHA-256. 1,536 questions are scored: categories 1 to 4, minus 4 with no evidence.
- 1 fresh AtticDB store per conversation. Each turn is 1 note: `"<speaker>: <text>"`.
- A question counts as found when one of its evidence turns, as labelled by the dataset, is in the top k. No answering model, no judge.
- The arms were fixed in `docs/plans/2026-11-07-locomo-retrieval.md` before any run.
- 1 run, seed 0, on an Apple M4 laptop with a load average of about 5 from other work. Latency is noisy. Recall doesn't depend on load.

## Results at k = 10

| Arm | Recall | Hit | ≈ tokens returned | Search p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AtticDB, whole question | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0 | 0.12 ms |
| AtticDB, rarest word | 39.0% | 44.0% | 191 | 0.11 ms |
| AtticDB, 2 rarest words | 27.5% | 30.9% | 70 | 0.10 ms |
| SQLite FTS5, BM25 over the question's words | 56.1% | 61.8% | 315 | 0.17 ms |
| Random 10 turns | 2.1% | 3.1% | 327 | n/a |

The full conversation averages about 19,200 tokens (bytes/4).

## What this says

- **Pasting the question finds nothing.** AtticDB search keeps only turns that hold every query word as a substring. A question has filler words, and its trailing "?" sticks to the last word. So 0 of 1,536 questions matched.
- **1 good word does much better.** The rarest word of the question finds evidence for 44% of questions in the top 10. Agents are told to search with 1 or 2 words, and this arm models that.
- **Ranking is the gap.** BM25 over the same words finds evidence for 62% in the top 10, 18 points more. AtticDB returns matches newest first, not best first. In a long conversation, the right turn is often older than 10 other matches.
- **2 words are worse than 1.** Requiring both words drops recall from 39% to 28%. The second word is often absent from the evidence turn.
- **Multi-hop and open-domain are hard for every arm here.** At k = 10, BM25 gets 27% recall on both. They need facts from several turns, or knowledge outside the chat. Word matching alone won't reach them.
- **Search is fast.** p50 is about 0.1 ms over the Unix socket, on the same machine. That isn't comparable to a hosted service's network round trip.

## Next to Living Brain

Living Brain reports 92.2% judged answer accuracy with GPT-5, 9.4K memory tokens per question and a 592 ms median retrieval ([benchmarks page](https://docs.livingbrain.com/docs/benchmarks)). This run measures something else: whether search returns the labelled evidence. Answer accuracy needs an answering model and a judge on top. Their page also reports no full-context accuracy, so the model's share of their score is unknown. Read these numbers as a floor for AtticDB's search, not a head-to-head.

## What this doesn't cover

- QA accuracy. An answering model might recover from a near-miss turn, or fail despite the right one.
- Semantic search. Embeddings are #117.
- Session dates. They weren't stored, so "when" questions rely on the turn text alone.
- Variance. It's 1 seed and 1 run. Every arm except random is deterministic.
