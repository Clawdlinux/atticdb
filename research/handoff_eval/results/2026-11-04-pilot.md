### What failed, by stage
- **Native memory doesn't cross tools.** In H4 native, Claude to Copilot, the Claude writer saved the rule in its own memory. That was `MEMORY.md`, with `bramble-ci` and `3.9`. The Copilot reader couldn't see it, so it answered "Yes" and failed. AtticDB exists to close exactly this gap.
- **atticdb arm: capture quality, not delivery.**
  - Delivery was 9/9, observed 2 ways.
  - Copilot readers (5): the reader's own `hook.end` events show the sessionStart block with the tokens.
  - Claude readers (4): delivery is inferred. A separate `atticdb hook` call, made before the session, returned the tokens.
  - In H1, both the Claude and the Copilot writer saved only the ticket, LEDG-7731. Neither saved the reason.
  - Both readers cited the ticket, so both pass under the plan's rule.
  - The reason (412 vs 24 bytes) was lost in capture, in both directions.
- **files arm: use.** In H1 files, Claude to Copilot, HANDOFF.md reached the reader. It held only LEDG-7731, and the reader didn't cite it. It gave a generic reason instead.
- **Abstain works.** No arm invented a retention period in H5.

### Gap to fix first
Capture quality. When an agent saves a decision, it should save the reason with its evidence.
Evidence means numbers, ticket ids and the constraint. When a decision is reversed, it should save what changed.
That's guidance in the MCP instructions, the tool descriptions and the chat-start block. It's not a storage change.
Then rerun this exact matrix with the same model pins and compare.
